[Development documentation](README.md)

## Coverage

The shared desktop and mobile presets contain 94 operation endpoints and 1,488 model records, including aliases and historical IDs. The public audit completed six probes. It had no scoped credential for 22 endpoints, no operation-specific catalog for 60, and no supported discovery protocol for six. Public inventories and documented capabilities establish provider availability. Private account entitlements, local installations and paid inference remain unverified.

## Chat and Decisions

- OpenAI's model-list API can attach a `shutdown_date` to an ID that still serves requests. Explicit catalog refreshes now expose that date in both clients' common descriptor and remove the model from recommendations. The audit reports the same announced date. GPT-5 and GPT-5.1, including their dated snapshots, remain available for explicit selection during their respective migration windows. [OpenAI model-list schema](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/models/methods/list), [OpenAI retirement schedule](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations), [GPT-5](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5), [GPT-5.1](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.1)
- Gemini 4 Neon is available on the paid Gemini API tier and for verified enterprise Vertex customers in global, US and EU locations. Both native routes allow four thinking levels with high as the default. The adapter maps a minimal request to low, accepts xhigh, and uses a 65,536-token synchronous output limit. Implicit caching begins at 4,096 tokens. OpenRouter independently advertises medium as the default and a 943,718-token output limit. Hydrogen remains Google's recommendation for everyday coding. [Gemini guide](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/whats-new-gemini-4-neon), [Vertex model card](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/4-neon), [OpenRouter model endpoints](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemini-4-neon/endpoints)
- MiniMax retired M2.5, M2.7 and both highspeed variants for pay-as-you-go keys on November 18. M Plan and Token Plan keys can still use these names to reach M3.1 Flash. The shared catalog keeps all four as account-dependent legacy aliases, excludes them from recommendations and identifies M3.1 Flash as their replacement. [MiniMax retirement notice](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/faq/models)
- OpenRouter continued to serve Kimi K2.5 through four providers and cleared the prior expiration date. The free Inkling 2 Small route still had one provider and an expiration date of November 19 at the time of the probe. Five routes returned lower output limits, and GLM-5.2 no longer advertised parallel tool calls. The final public comparison found no missing or new chat IDs. It reported larger top-provider limits than the bundled values for MiMo V2.5 Pro and DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731. The former has varied between reads, and the latter has providers with different limits. The bundled fallbacks retain the lower previously observed top-provider capacities. [OpenRouter catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models), [Kimi endpoints](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/moonshotai/kimi-k2.5/endpoints), [DeepSeek endpoints](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731/endpoints)
- SiliconFlow China's public catalog continued to list Qwen3 Coder 30B with normal service status after its announced deprecation. The Gemini Developer API lists 3.6 Flash without a shutdown date. Vertex schedules the same ID for retirement on November 19. These independent regional lifecycles remain visible in the presets. [SiliconFlow inventory](https://www.siliconflow.cn/models), [Gemini deprecations](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/deprecations), [Vertex lifecycle](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/model-versions)
- The OpenRouter Decisions inventory matched the catalog. The TypeSafe, OpenAI, SiliconFlow CN and international, and Alibaba CN and international operation guides were reviewed. Regional credentials and optional deployments still determine actual availability. [OpenRouter Decisions](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=decisions&endpoints=/api/alpha/decisions), [TypeSafe](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models), [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions), [SiliconFlow CN](https://docs.siliconflow.cn/docs/api/systemone-post), [SiliconFlow international](https://docs.siliconflow.com/en/api-reference/systemone/create-systemone), [Alibaba](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/model-studio/decision-model-api)

## Follow-up catalog review

The second pass validated 1,510 shared records across the same 94 endpoints. The six public inventories returned 480 chat, 70 image, 43 decision, 35 video and 38 embedding records from OpenRouter, plus 30 records from Jina. Comparison by operation found no added or missing selectable IDs. The two OpenRouter output-limit differences described above persisted. Twenty-two endpoints required credentials, 60 lacked an operation-specific inventory in the auditor and six used other discovery protocols. Both subscription runtimes continue to obtain the signed-in user's choices from their host. [OpenRouter models](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models), [Jina models](https://api.jina.ai/v1/models), [Copilot model availability](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/reference/ai-models/supported-models)
